# Reference: Convergent Thinking

## Purpose
Reduce the option space responsibly and identify the strongest supported option, shortlist or experiment.

## 1. Evidence
Check:
- Source and provenance.
- Relevance to this specific problem.
- Recency.
- Reliability and potential bias.
- Supporting and contradicting evidence.
- What the evidence cannot establish.

Questions:
- What evidence supports this?
- What would disconfirm it?
- Is this evidence direct or indirect?
- Are we generalising beyond the sample?

## 2. Facts
Classify statements:
- Verified fact.
- User-provided fact (not independently verified).
- Disputed statement.
- Estimate.
- Assumption.
- Interpretation.
- Unknown.

Questions:
- What do we know?
- How do we know it?
- What is being inferred?
- What remains unverified?

## 3. Data
Check:
- Baseline and target.
- Numerator and denominator.
- Sample size and representativeness.
- Measurement period.
- Missing values and data quality.
- Trends and variability.
- Whether the measure reflects the real outcome.

Do not fabricate missing figures. Use ranges or qualitative descriptions only when justified and label estimates.

## 4. Analysis
Examine:
- Benefits and expected impact.
- Cost and resource demand.
- Feasibility.
- Dependencies.
- Risks and mitigations.
- Short- and long-term effects.
- Direct and indirect consequences.
- Stakeholder impact.
- Reversibility.

## 5. Reasoning
Test:
- Does the evidence support the proposed mechanism?
- Are there alternative explanations?
- Is the causal chain plausible?
- Are intermediate steps missing?
- What conditions must hold?
- Does the conclusion overreach?

## 6. Logic
Look for:
- Contradictions.
- False dichotomies.
- Circular reasoning.
- Hasty generalisation.
- Appeal to authority.
- Post hoc reasoning.
- Correlation treated as causation.
- Moving goalposts.
- Inconsistent standards.

## Convergent output
For each shortlisted option:
1. Strongest supporting case.
2. Strongest opposing case.
3. Evidence quality.
4. Feasibility and constraints.
5. Risks and trade-offs.
6. Critical assumptions.
7. Conditions for success.
8. Reasons to reject or defer.

## Convergent quality tests
- Are criteria explicit?
- Are fatal constraints treated as gates rather than averaged away?
- Are all serious options evaluated consistently?
- Is uncertainty visible?
- Is the recommendation proportionate to the evidence?
