# Worked Example: Reducing Delays in an Internal Approval Process

> Illustrative example only. The context and figures below are fictional and must not be treated as real-world evidence.

## 1. Problem
A team reports that internal approvals take too long. No baseline or root-cause data has yet been provided.

## 2. Clarification needed
- What approval type and process?
- What is the current end-to-end cycle time?
- Where does waiting occur?
- How many cases are affected?
- What controls are mandatory?
- What outcome matters: speed, quality, compliance or all three?

Because these facts are missing, do not claim that the approval process itself is the root cause.

## 3. Divergent exploration

### New angle
Maybe the delay is caused by incomplete submissions rather than approver availability.

### What if?
What if low-risk cases did not require the same approval path as high-risk cases, subject to policy approval?

### Another idea
Create a submission checklist and standard request format.

### Unexpected option
Use office hours or pre-submission coaching to resolve common questions before formal submission.

### Fresh perspective
Requesters may experience the process as confusing; approvers may experience submissions as incomplete.

### Different approach
Revisit whether every approval step is necessary, while preserving required controls.

## 4. Convergent evaluation
Evidence is currently insufficient. Collect timestamps for submission, clarification, queue entry, review and decision. Sample enough cases to understand variation. Check rework and error rates, not just elapsed time.

Potential options:
- Checklist and standard form.
- Better routing.
- Risk-tiered approval, only if authorised.
- Remove redundant steps, only after control review.

## 5. Lateral synthesis
A coherent initial package might combine a standard request form, clearer routing and a weekly review of bottlenecks. Risk-tiered routing remains a hypothesis until policy owners validate it.

## 6. Decision status
**Experiment first.** There is not enough evidence to recommend a structural redesign.

## 7. Small test
Pilot the standard form and routing guidance with a defined request category. Compare cycle time, rework and control exceptions against a baseline. Agree success thresholds before the pilot begins.
