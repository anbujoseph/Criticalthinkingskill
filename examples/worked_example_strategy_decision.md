# Worked Example: Choosing a Growth Route

> Illustrative example only. No company facts, market data or financial projections are asserted.

## 1. Problem
A hypothetical small business wants to grow but has not yet defined whether growth means revenue, profit, customer reach, recurring income or market share.

## 2. Clarifying questions
- What is the primary growth outcome?
- Which customer segment is being served?
- What is the current offer and evidence of demand?
- What capacity and budget exist?
- What level of risk is acceptable?
- What is the time horizon?

Do not choose a channel or growth strategy before these questions are answered.

## 3. Divergent options
- **New angle:** Improve retention and customer value before acquiring more customers.
- **What if?:** What if growth came from partnerships rather than direct acquisition?
- **Another idea:** Add a related service for existing customers.
- **Unexpected option:** License a method or toolkit rather than deliver every service directly.
- **Fresh perspective:** Examine the buyer's procurement process and the end user's experience separately.
- **Different approach:** Narrow the target segment and create a repeatable offer rather than broaden the portfolio.

## 4. Convergent evaluation
For each option, examine:
- Evidence of customer need and willingness to pay.
- Verified customer and financial facts.
- Acquisition, retention, margin and capacity data.
- Delivery feasibility and opportunity cost.
- Causal reasoning between the proposed activity and growth.
- Logical consistency between the growth goal and chosen measure.

## 5. Lateral synthesis
A possible sequence could be: validate one narrow offer with existing customers, improve repeatability, then test a partner channel. This is a hypothesis, not a recommendation until customer and capacity evidence is available.

## 6. Decision status
**Clarify / experiment first.** Define the growth metric and test demand before committing to a major expansion.
